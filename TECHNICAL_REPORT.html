Solution branches

Five ways to solve the same handling task

Every branch starts with the same camera, product track, robot limits, PLC gates, and evidence recorder. A and B are implemented. C, D, and E are researched extensions, not completed simulator results.

A | LIVE

Direct delivery

The robot picks from the moving belt, turns the product, and releases it on the cutter-entry tray. It is the shortest route and the first performance baseline.

Best when the first pose estimate is trustworthy and the cutter is ready.

B | LIVE

Buffered correction

The robot places the product in a buffer, looks at it again, and makes a corrected second grasp before delivery.

It costs time and handling, but gives slip or pose change a second correction opportunity.

C | DISCUSS

Learned grasp score

A small RGBD model could rank several safe grasp points by predicted contact, retained lift, slip, and delivery quality.

This is a discussion option. It is not built or tested.

D | DISCUSS

Reactive intercept

The robot could keep correcting the chosen target during approach until a defined no-return time.

This is a discussion option. It is not built or tested.

E | DISCUSS

Bounded manipulation skill

An imitation or residual policy could learn the hard contact segment, such as close, stabilize, correct slip, and place.

This is a discussion option. It is not built or tested.

Accuracy benchmark

Where error enters the cycle

Fifteen headless Isaac runs varied speed, lateral position, yaw, camera latency, and injected vision noise. Ten normal-envelope cases form the release gate. Five harder cases show where the current simulated method degrades.

Core distribution

A camera error was 5.08 mm mean and 6.66 mm p95. B was 4.30 mm mean and 5.73 mm p95. Final placement was 12.70 mm mean for A and 15.98 mm for B.

Stress boundary

The 0.30 m/s, 80 mm offset, 85 degree case was rejected as too late and recovered safely. Two high-noise cases delivered the workpiece but exceeded the 2 degree camera and tracking yaw gates.

Repeatability

Exact cross-process equality did not hold. Both repeated seeds passed bounded replay. The largest placement delta was 0.067 mm. Solution B timing differed by 3.43 ms.

Internal simulation gates

  • Camera and tracked position mean at or below 12 mm.
  • Camera and tracked yaw mean at or below 2 degrees.
  • Intercept position at or below 15 mm and yaw at or below 3 degrees.
  • Delivery position at or below 55 mm and angle at or below 7 degrees.

How to read this

  • Ground truth scores the output after each run. It is not fed to control.
  • A functional delivery can still fail an accuracy gate.
  • These thresholds are regression limits, not production requirements.
  • Real camera and workpiece trials are required next.

Discussion only

Learning options for a later phase

C, D, and E are not being built now. They show where learning could help if A and B expose repeatable limits in simulation and later hardware tests.

C | GRASP

Candidate ranking

Generate several safe mask-interior grasps. Train a small RGBD or point-cloud model to predict bilateral contact, retained lift, slip, excessive contact, and delivery quality.

A/B test against the current geometric selector on held-out cuts, speeds, yaw, latency, friction, and compliance.

D | MOTION

Target-referenced servo

Track the same chosen contact region across new frames and apply capped Cartesian corrections until the no-return time.

A/B test against predict-once motion under belt ramps, encoder bias, latency spikes, and small disturbances.

E | SKILL

Imitation or residual control

Use behavior cloning, ACT, Diffusion Policy, or residual reinforcement learning for the close, stabilize, slip-correct, and place segment.

Start in replay and shadow mode. Promote only after physical demonstrations and held-out rollout evidence exist.

The full research report compares all five routes and cites GraspNet, Dex-Net, Contact-GraspNet, VGN, AnyGrasp, reactive grasping, Robomimic, ACT, Diffusion Policy, DAgger, residual reinforcement learning, Isaac Lab, MoveIt, cuMotion, OpenVLA, Octo, tactile slip work, and food-handling research.

Decision record

What we chose, why, and what the tests said

These are engineering choices, not immutable truths. Each one is tied to an implemented artifact or a named validation gap.

Deterministic first cell

A one-month prototype needs visible timing, recovery, and machine boundaries.

Implemented typed contracts, fixed-step time, explicit state machines, rejection rules, and PLC gates.

Stationary cutter-entry tray

The downstream transfer mechanism was not specified well enough to invent a moving feed.

The robot aligns to cut_target_frame, releases, and verifies near-zero product speed.

FANUC six-axis reference

A standard industrial arm gives the scene a real kinematic basis and realistic reach constraints.

Six joints, imported meshes, limits, inertia, Lula IK, and Isaac articulation control ran. OEM dynamics remain unproven.

YOLO mask, not box center

Irregular workpieces need boundary clearance and jaw orientation.

The mask-aware proposal drives the overlay, planner, jaw width, tool yaw, and placement compensation.

Bilateral contact as grasp proof

A close command cannot prove pickup.

Both fingers must contact the same workpiece before lift. The same rigid body reaches the tray with no post-grasp pose writes.

Keep A and B

A minimizes motion. B adds a second observation and regrasp.

Both passed all core cases. A had lower mean delivery error in the current small matrix. B remains valuable as a correction route.

Fail closed

A late, stale, or uncertain target should not become a false pick.

The extreme 0.30 m/s case was rejected before grasp. Recovery cases produced their expected terminal states.

Bounded replay, not exact replay

Separate Isaac processes did not produce bit-identical floating-point results.

A and B passed numerical replay tolerances. The largest repeated placement delta was 0.067 mm.

The complete record of decisions, implementation, A/B tests, results, and open physical parameters is in DECISIONS_AND_TESTS.md.

Recovery evidence

Failures have expected safe outcomes

A fault run passes when the controller detects the condition, avoids false delivery, reaches the required recovery state, and records matching evidence.

Failed grasp

No bilateral contact. Open, retreat, recover.

Cutter unavailable

No permissive. Route attached product to reject.

Stale observation

Reject the plan before commitment.

Emergency stop

Stop conveyor, hold, enter safe stop.

Nominal cycle passes when

  • Rendered RGB and depth are valid and calibrated.
  • YOLO and tracking stay current.
  • Motion completes without limit or collision violations.
  • Both fingers confirm contact and retention.
  • Alignment, release, and PLC acknowledgement pass.

Nominal cycle fails when

  • No valid proposal arrives before the deadline.
  • Timing, reach, uncertainty, or clearance is unsafe.
  • Contact or retention is lost.
  • The cutter is unavailable or emergency stop is active.
  • Delivery evidence or artifact consistency fails.

Validation boundary

What remains unproven

The simulation is a runnable engineering baseline. It is not a substitute for workpiece trials, real sensing, robot commissioning, machine integration, or safety validation.

Robot and cell assets

The FANUC asset is a description reference, not an OEM-certified digital twin. Other cell equipment is project geometry.

Product physics

Workpieces are rigid. Deformation, moisture, adhesion, tearing, pressure, and sanitation effects are absent.

Grasp mechanics

Finger effort, contact impulses, friction, and compliance are uncalibrated proxies.

Perception

The model uses synthetic imagery. Real glare, fluids, occlusion, blur, and domain shift remain unmeasured.

Machine timing

Cutter phase, feed speed, encoder behavior, permissives, and faults need real PLC captures.

External motion stack

ROS 2 JointTrajectory ran. Live MoveIt FollowJointTrajectory execution was unavailable.

Next engineering task

Collect real camera, calibration, workpiece, grasp, cutter PLC, and OEM limit data. Then commission the external ROS 2 and MoveIt controller path.
