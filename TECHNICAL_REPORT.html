Solution B adds a buffer, a second RGBD observation, and slip correction.

The state machine reads Isaac Sim state. A YOLO result never moves the arm by itself. Before the robot moves, the planner checks time, uncertainty, reach, motion limits, and cutter readiness.

Solution A and Solution B

Both share the same perception, tracking, prediction, interception, articulation, grasp, PLC, logging, and recovery contracts. They differ only after grasp verification.

Solution A

Direct tray loading

The robot carries the intercepted workpiece directly to cut_target_frame and releases it into the stationary cutter-entry tray. If the cutter cannot guarantee a permissive before commitment, the controller rejects or diverts.

  • Fewer handoffs
  • Direct alignment and stationary release
  • More sensitive to cutter availability
  • Injected failure: cutter unavailable before commit
Solution B

Buffered re-observation and tray loading

The robot places the workpiece in a centering buffer and lets it settle. A new camera image measures any pose change. The robot corrects the target, grips the workpiece again, and loads the stationary tray when the cutter is ready.

  • Decouples interception from cutter timing
  • Allows post-grasp pose correction
  • Adds occupancy and maximum-hold contracts
  • Injected failure: buffer timeout

Frames, timing, motion, and grasp

Every pose names its coordinate frame and every observation keeps its capture time. The robot aims for the predicted future workpiece pose, not the last detected pixel location.

Named coordinate frames

world, robot_base, conveyor, belt_surface, camera, camera_calibration_target, meat_track, grasp_track, tool0, buffer_frame, cut_target_frame, and cutter_feed_frame.

Camera world translation is [1.0, 0.0, 3.0] m. The belt surface is at world Z = 0.04 m in the accepted reference scene.

Fixed-step time

Physics runs at 240 Hz. Camera exposure is 60 Hz. The recorder samples at 12 FPS. Observations retain exposure time after delivery latency. The tracker and planner receive simulation time explicitly. No domain function owns a wall clock.

Setup validation measured exactly 240 domain steps for one simulated second.

Motion limits and interception

Every move is a preflight-checked quintic trajectory. X, Y, Z, yaw, and finger joints have explicit position, velocity, and acceleration bounds. TCP targets are checked against the validated work envelope and cutter exclusion geometry.

The X trajectory reaches the predicted interception pose while matching the 2.24 m/s belt velocity. A bounded braking move stops conveyor-relative motion before lift.

Contact, compliance, and slip

Each finger reports PhysX contact. A grasp is accepted only after recent contact on both sides. Finite-effort prismatic drives and soft-pad material properties provide a bounded compliance proxy. After confirmation, the rigid workpiece remains attached through simulated contact and friction. Its pose is not written by the control code.

Solution B releases on the buffer, renders a new observation, estimates the commanded-to-observed slip transform, corrects it, and requires new contact before feed.

Failure handling

Failures are typed, traced, and connected to physical recovery motion. Gates are not weakened to make injected fault cycles look successful.

Failed grasp

If both finger contacts are not confirmed, no hold is accepted. The controller enters recovery, opens the gripper, moves to a known-safe pose, and records failed_grasp_contact_confirmation.

Cutter unavailable

Solution A refuses direct commitment when readiness cannot be guaranteed. An attached product follows the reject route. The PLC fault and terminal reason are traced.

Buffer timeout

Solution B enforces buffer occupancy and maximum hold time. If readiness does not arrive, it executes recovery and records buffer_timeout.

Slip correction

Buffer re-observation compares commanded and observed product pose. A detected transform changes the regrasp target. New two-finger contact is required.

Emergency stop

The PLC stops the conveyor and transitions to SAFE_STOP. A limit-clamped measured hold command goes through the articulation controller. Drift is measured before recovery.

Stale observation

The clock advances until the observation exceeds its validity. Planning rejects it before commit and executes the explicit reject or recovery path.

Where the implementation lives

The core control code can run in focused unit tests. The Isaac adapter translates between that code and simulator objects. Only the integrated runner counts as evidence for a full simulated cycle.

isaac_sim/scene2_builder.py

Authors the visible USD cell, FANUC description reference, compact gripper, physics, sensors, frames, guards, buffer, cutter, and PLC attributes.

isaac_sim/run_scene2_integrated.py

Runs the final fixed-step RGBD, YOLO26, tracking, prediction, Lula IK, articulation control, contact grasp, A or B flow, PLC, recovery, stage reload, video, traces, and metrics.

tools/audit_scene2_integrated.py

Fails closed on missing USD content, reload evidence, sensor output, learned perception, commands, contact, lift, retention, delivery tolerance, state order, video, or trace evidence.

configs/fanuc_m10id12_lula.yaml

Defines the standard-arm kinematic chain, joint ordering, c-space limits, acceleration limits, and collision spheres used by the Lula solver.

isaac_sim/yolo_perception.py

Runs YOLO26 masks on rendered RGB and combines them with depth and calibration to produce the domain observation contract.

isaac_sim/video_recorder.py

Streams actual RGB24 camera frames into H.264 with ffmpeg and records frame, timing, codec, and file metadata.

meatcell/contracts.py

Defines versioned transforms, timestamps, observations, tracks, plans, cutter state, events, and terminal results.

meatcell/tracking.py

Associates observations, maintains lifecycle and covariance, fuses conveyor speed, and predicts future state.

meatcell/interception.py

Evaluates candidate times and rejects stale, late, uncertain, unreachable, or unsafe plans.

meatcell/grasp.py

Separates capture, hold, damage proxy, slip estimate, and corrected product pose.

meatcell/grasp_selection.py

Classifies product orientation and selects a central high-clearance image point from the YOLO mask. It publishes the typed grasp proposal used by the robot.

meatcell/trajectory.py

Validates and samples standard six-joint trajectories using simulation time. The ROS bridge accepts JointTrajectory messages; live MoveIt action execution remains outside the tested boundary.

meatcell/solutions.py

Implements Solution A direct feed, Solution B buffer flow, delivery tolerances, and PLC permissive sequence handling.

meatcell/supervisor.py

Enforces legal state transitions and explicit success, reject, recovered, safe-stop, and partial terminal paths.

Assumptions and unresolved physical parameters

You can run the simulation with the assumptions below. The next phase must replace those assumptions with measurements from the real process.

Reference asset assumptions

The FANUC M-10iD/12 model uses an official-description reference geometry and kinematic description, but it is not an OEM-certified digital twin. The compact gripper, rigid workpiece, cutter station, conveyor, guards, and buffer are project reference geometry.

Contact assumptions

The jaw geometry, 50 N closure setpoint, 70 N effort limit, 0.00016 m/N compliance proxy, friction, and PhysX contact impulses are uncalibrated. Tissue deformation, wet adhesion, pressure distribution, tearing, and sanitation effects are not modeled.

Perception assumptions

The model sees synthetic appearance, lighting, geometry, and camera characteristics. Real lens calibration, motion blur, fluids, glare, occlusion, contamination, and domain shift remain unmeasured.

Machine and safety assumptions

Cutter phase, permissive timing, encoder behavior, robot dynamics, stopping performance, guards, and emergency-stop behavior require real I/O captures, OEM limits, and qualified safety engineering.

Exact next integration task

Commission the external ROS 2 and MoveIt FollowJointTrajectory stack against an authorized controller environment. In parallel, collect real camera data, calibration, conveyor measurements, workpiece grasp tests, cutter PLC timing, and OEM controller limits.
