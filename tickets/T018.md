# Solution D closed-loop reactive interception

Update the same tracked product and grasp target during approach until a defined no-return time. Apply bounded Cartesian or joint corrections below the existing deterministic execution gates.

## Inputs

- the committed track ID and selected C or geometric grasp proposal
- new timestamped rendered RGBD observations and conveyor encoder samples
- measured FANUC joint state, TCP state, remaining contact time, and current trajectory
- correction bounds, no-return time, collision margin, and PLC and emergency-stop state

## Outputs

- same-identity target updates
- bounded Cartesian or joint correction proposals with age and sequence number
- applied, ignored, fallback, reject, or safe-stop decision and reason
- correction count, magnitude, latency, residual intercept error, and clearance evidence

## Data source

Use complete Scene 2 runs with rendered RGBD, moving conveyor, measured articulation state, actual controller commands, PhysX contact, compliant gripper, and PLC transitions. Perturb belt speed ramps, encoder bias, observation latency spikes, yaw or pose disturbances, and target freshness.

## Training or fitting method

The first slice may use a fitted residual gain or deterministic receding-horizon correction. If any learned residual is used, fit it only from complete simulator trajectories and retain held-out seeds. The key comparison is reactive execution against the predict-once baseline, not offline prediction alone.

## Inference boundary

Updates must retain track and grasp identity. They stop at the no-return boundary. Every correction passes freshness, timing, reach, joint, velocity, acceleration, singularity, workspace, collision, PLC, and emergency-stop checks before execution.

## Fallback behavior

Ignore an invalid update and continue the last safe committed trajectory only when its original gates remain valid. Otherwise reject or safe-stop. Never chase a new product or grasp after commitment.

## Pass criteria

- C is complete and remains green.
- Focused tests cover identity retention, correction caps, no-return behavior, stale updates, oscillation limits, fallback, reject, and emergency stop.
- Complete A and B Scene 2 comparisons cover belt ramps, encoder bias, latency spikes, and pose disturbances.
- Reactive execution reduces held-out intercept error or raises grasp and delivery success without new collision, motion-limit, PLC, recovery, or cycle-time gate failures.
- Evidence includes RGBD, target-update visualization, corrections, trajectory, contact, PLC, trace, video, USD, metrics, audit, and bounded replay.

## Fail criteria

- The controller switches product or grasp identity after commitment.
- Corrections continue after no-return time or bypass a deterministic gate.
- A proxy-only test is used for an integrated claim.
- The comparison does not beat or safely match predict-once under the defined perturbations.

## Exact simulator evidence

- per-update JSONL with observation age, target sequence, correction, applied gate results, and measured state
- paired seeded D0 and D1 full-cell artifacts for every perturbation family
- machine-readable comparison with intercept error, timing, correction count, minimum clearance, grasp, delivery, cycle time, recovery, collision, motion limits, and replay
- one-command D launcher and documented release command
