What the cell must do

A prepared, separated meat cut arrives on a fast conveyor. The cell must see it, predict where it will be, intercept it, grip it, orient it, and release it into a stationary cutter-entry tray.

The robot has one job: take a prepared meat cut from the moving belt and place it in the cutter-entry tray in the right position.

Selected boundary

The source belt moves at a nominal 2.24 m/s. The tray stays still. After release, the cutter controls the guarded feed and blade motion.

Design principle

YOLO estimates the workpiece outline from the camera image. Conventional control code handles tracking, prediction, timing, motion limits, permissives, and recovery.

See

Render RGB and depth from the fixed sensor mount.

Track

Fuse detections, timestamps, and encoder motion.

Predict

Choose a reachable pose and interception time.

Grip

Match belt velocity and confirm bilateral contact.

Align

Reorient the product to cut_target_frame.

Release

Place into the stationary tray only when permissive.

How the parts communicate

Perception produces observations. It never commands the arm directly. The deterministic controller combines observations, encoder state, robot state, and PLC permissives.

Inputs to the cell controller

  • vision_observationMask, pose, confidence, track ID, exposure time
  • encoder_stateBelt position, belt velocity, timestamp, quality
  • robot_stateJoint position, velocity, tool pose, controller time
  • gripper_stateJaw position, contact, force proxy, slip estimate
  • machine_stateRecipe, cutter ready, tray clear, phase, faults
  • safety_stateGuard, emergency stop, drive permissive

Outputs from the cell controller

  • target_reservationTrack ID, intercept pose, intercept time, expiry
  • joint_trajectoryTime-indexed positions with velocity and acceleration limits
  • gripper_commandOpen position, close position, force limit, release
  • machine_handshakeRobot ready, product delivered, result code, acknowledgment
  • recovery_commandReject, retract, measured-position hold, safe stop
  • cycle_traceStamped observations, transitions, commands, contacts, metrics

What 30 ms costs at belt speed

At 2.24 m/s, the workpiece moves 67.2 mm in 30 ms. Camera timestamps, encoder timing, and calibration can therefore move the planned grasp by several finger widths.

Travel during unmodeled latency

distance = 2.24 m/s x latency

Every observation, encoder sample, robot state, PLC transition, and command must use one simulation clock and an explicit acquisition timestamp.

Control sequence and recovery

The full state machine runs against simulator state. A nominal cycle and every recovery path use the same articulation, contact, and PLC interfaces.

ObserveAcquire RGB, depth, encoder, and exposure time.
TrackAssign identity and estimate position, yaw, and velocity.
PredictForecast the future product pose inside the pick window.
ReserveReject stale, uncertain, crowded, late, or unreachable targets.
ApproachPlan a collision-checked joint trajectory to the pre-grasp pose.
InterceptArrive at the predicted pose and match belt velocity.
Confirm graspClose compliant jaws and require bilateral recent contact.
Lift and stabilizeBrake relative motion and estimate slip from the tool transform.
AlignReorient to the recipe-specific cut_target_frame.
ReleaseRequire cutter ready and tray clear before opening.
Verify and retractMeasure tray pose, acknowledge the result, and return home.
Recover or rejectUse bounded retract, reject placement, or measured-position hold.
Emergency stopStop the belt and hold the measured joint state in simulation.
